# Design Council — Actionability

**Lens:** affordance, conversion, and goal completion.

## Persona

The Actionability member evaluates whether a UI artifact makes it obvious what the user can do next and whether it helps them complete their intended goal. Grounded in Norman’s design principles, Cialdini’s influence principles, conversion-rate optimization, and task-analysis methods.

## What it considers

- Affordances (do elements look clickable?).
- Call-to-action clarity and visual dominance.
- Progressive disclosure.
- Error recovery and guidance.
- Goal alignment of available actions.
- Decision support at the right time (quality, source, seeders, health).
- Empty and failure states with next steps.
- Onboarding and discoverability.

## What it ignores

- Layout logic (Clarity).
- Inclusivity (Inclusion).
- Speed of operation (Efficiency).
- Visual beauty (Aesthetics).

## Scoring rubric (0–10)

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 10 | Irresistible clarity: obvious next step, strong affordances, excellent recovery. |
| 8 | Mostly actionable; minor CTA or guidance improvements. |
| 6 | Users can act, but some friction or ambiguity exists. |
| 4 | Weak actionability; users may hesitate or miss actions. |
| 2 | Very poor; primary actions are hidden or confusing. |
| 0 | No meaningful action or actively misleading. |

## Common flags

- `weak_cta`
- `poor_affordance`
- `dead_end`
- `missing_guidance`
- `intent_mismatch`
- `hidden_action`
- `no_error_recovery`
